Exploitation ReportNovember 18, 2026

Executive Summary

A now-patched flaw in Apple's audio service (CVE-2026-65339) allowed apps on iPhones and iPads to record audio even after users denied microphone permission. The bypass worked through the audiomxd service accepting a forged audit token that impersonated the audio service itself, permitting recording without TCC approval and, on some builds, without the orange microphone indicator appearing. Security updates shipped between August and October 2026 across iOS, iPadOS, macOS, tvOS, watchOS, and visionOS. Neither Apple nor the researchers reported observed exploitation, and CISA's known exploited vulnerabilities catalog does not list the flaw; its exploitation status remains unknown.

A critical-severity flaw in the ScreenConnect client (CVE-2026-84869, CVSS 9.9) could allow files to be transferred and executed through an active remote session without authorization or Host confirmation. ConnectWise addressed the issue in version 26.6.5 and advised on-premises partners to disable identified third-party extensions and update immediately. Unknown threat actors are suspected of leveraging related authentication flaws to deploy the SCKit toolkit, including a worm-like technique that attempts to execute malicious code on technician workstations; however, there is no evidence that worm-like behavior succeeded in the latest campaign. Exploitation status is assessed as potential.

Affected Systems and Products

  • Apple iOS and iPadOS: iPhone 11 and later, iPad Pro 12.9-inch 3rd generation and later, iPad Pro 11-inch 1st generation and later, iPad Air 3rd generation and later, iPad 8th generation and later, iPad mini 5th generation and later (iOS 26.6.1 / iPadOS 26.6.1); iPhone XS, iPhone XS Max, iPhone XR, and iPad 7th generation (iOS 18.7.11 / iPadOS 18.7.11)
  • Apple macOS: macOS Tahoe 26.6.2 and macOS Sequoia 15.8
  • Apple tvOS, watchOS, and visionOS: tvOS 27 for Apple TV 4K 2nd generation and later; watchOS 27 for Apple Watch Series 9 and later; visionOS 27 for Apple Vision Pro (all models)
  • ScreenConnect on-premises environments: Technician clients running versions prior to 26.6.5 where identified third-party extensions remain enabled

Attack Vectors and Techniques

  • Audit token impersonation: An app sends the audiomxd audio service an audit token naming audiomxd itself, causing the service to treat the app as its own internal process and skip TCC permission checks for microphone recording.
  • ScreenConnect remote session file execution: Through CVE-2026-84869, files can be transferred and executed through an active remote session without authorization or Host confirmation; SCKit malware uses this in a worm-like technique when a technician connects to an infected device.
  • Cloud-service C2 abuse: GitDoor and GraphDoor backdoors use GitHub repositories and Microsoft SharePoint/Graph API for command-and-control, proxying traffic through modified Erfrp clients and Ably messaging to blend with legitimate enterprise cloud traffic.
  • Microsoft 365 session token theft: Modern phishing kits (EvilTokens, Knight Office, Kali365) use device code phishing, adversary-in-the-middle relaying, and token replay to capture and reuse authenticated Microsoft 365 sessions without requiring credentials.
  • Compromised Python package supply chain: Malicious agent-framework versions 1.26.1 and 1.26.2 drop .pth files that execute credential-stealing code on every Python interpreter start, exfiltrating Azure credentials, tokens, and environment variables to an external webhook.

Threat Actor Activities

  • Operation ShadowCloud (GitDoor and GraphDoor): An active intrusion campaign identified by Kaspersky in early 2026 targeting government sectors in Asia and Africa; the threat actor's identity is unknown but shows tactical overlap with China-nexus activity tracked as Bronze Sandcastle, as well as similarities to FamousSparrow and CopperTurtle (BlackTech). At least one unspecified Taiwanese organization was targeted.
  • Cold Relic (COLDRIVER / UNC4057 / Callisto / Gossamer Bear / Star Blizzard): A Russia-linked espionage group that compromised a U.S. organization's cloud productivity environment in late 2025, then used stolen Super Admin credentials, MFA device registration, and Google Takeout to exfiltrate data before pivoting to compromise a second U.S. organization via Microsoft Entra ID federated SSO.
  • Trigram campaign: A large-scale operation impersonating about 50 AI and blockchain brands targeting developers across Europe and the U.S.; assessed with high confidence as a continuation of the UNK_DeadDrop cluster linked to North Korea, using recruitment, conference, and package-tracking lures to deploy the Overlord RAT and other infostealers.
  • Unknown SCKit actors: Unidentified threat actors exploiting ScreenConnect authentication flaws to deploy the multi-component SCKit toolkit, including RelayTrap for C#-based persistence, impacting 205 hosts across 41 organizations in activity detected November 13, 2026.
  • Cold Relic follow-on social engineering: After ejection from the first victim, the actor used email accounts impersonating employees to conduct renewed social engineering campaigns against internal employees and professional associates, changing messaging app profile names to new personas to target additional organizations.
