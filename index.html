Executive Summary

The most significant campaign activity involves Operation ShadowCloud, an active intrusion attributed by Kaspersky to an unknown actor with assessed China-nexus overlaps. Government sectors in Asia and Africa were targeted using the GraphDoor and GitDoor backdoors, which route command-and-control traffic through legitimate services such as Microsoft SharePoint, GitHub, and Ably. Initial access was linked to MeshCentral/MeshAgent activity, but no CVE IDs are supplied for these backdoors, and the scoped exploitation assessment for associated findings remains unknown.

Recent patching activity includes N-able N-central Hotfix 3, which addresses 17 flaws and removes legacy API mode, and separate reporting that 4EverAble2Shell flaws have been actively exploited to deploy cryptocurrency miners. ScreenConnect CVE-2026-84869 is patched in client version 26.6.5 but carries a potential exploitation assessment because SCKit malware attempts a worm-like file-transfer technique through remote sessions. Chrome CVE-2026-125350 is patched in recent stable-channel updates, while the scoped exploitation status for this CVE is unknown. Langflow CVE-2025-3248 and RAGFlow CVE-2026-28797 appear in AI-agent playbook context with patch or mitigation guidance, but their scoped exploitation statuses are unknown.

AI-driven threat activity is a recurring theme: Google GTIG and the FBI describe ARTEX, CAIRN, and related frameworks automating vulnerability discovery and patch reverse-engineering, while Barracuda tied a cloud breach to a stolen AI vendor key and ARTEX usage. Separate incidents involve Optimizely-supplied malicious JavaScript delivering crypto clippers, card skimmers, and iOS exploit kits, Fandango checkout payment-card theft, and unpatched Danfoss refrigeration-controller flaws for which agencies advised removing internet-facing management interfaces. These are reported as incidents, trends, or mitigations rather than confirmed exploitation of every cited CVE.

Affected versions unresolved. Review the linked reporting before inferring an affected version range.

Vendor guidance: bsi.bund.de, ccb.belgium.be, ncsc.gov.ie, thehackernews.com

  • Description: Six flaws in Danfoss AK-SM800A refrigeration controllers could allow remote takeover without login; no patch is available and firmware 5.4.0 does not fix the flaws.
  • Impact: Remote code execution as root; change controller settings, start defrost cycles, hide activity from logs, plant code surviving factory reset or firmware update, or render the controller unusable.
  • Status: No patch available. Belgium, Ireland and Germany advised removing management interfaces from the internet. Exploitation of the six new flaws has not been observed or linked to earlier refrigeration-controller incidents, although controllers were targeted in attacks in France and across Europe.
  • Affected Versions: Unresolved from supplied sources.
  • Exceptions: The German agency initially stated 5.4.0 was not affected but later corrected that it cannot rule out for now that 5.4.0 is affected.
  • Recommended Actions: Find every controller whose management interface is reachable from the internet, including through a 4G or 5G modem, and remove that exposure; give remote access only through a secure VPN or protected internal network; check temperature setpoints, defrost schedules, alarm settings, user accounts and remote-access settings for unauthorized changes; if compromise is suspected, change all credentials and verify connected equipment works properly.

Affected Systems and Products

  • ScreenConnect client and RMM extensions: On-premises ScreenConnect technician client environments with identified third-party extensions enabled; ConnectWise advises technician client version 26.6.5 or later and disabling identified extensions until further guidance.
  • N-central RMM/UEM: On-premise N-central servers require Hotfix 3; hosted servers are updated automatically; all Agents and Probes must be upgraded because non-upgraded Agents and Probes are susceptible to impersonation. Related 4EverAble2Shell activity deployed cryptocurrency miners on vulnerable N-central servers.
  • Langflow and RAGFlow AI platforms: Internet-exposed AI app builders and gateways; Langflow fixed version 1.3.0; RAGFlow advisory lists no patched version, with 0.25.0 sandbox rendering noted in SSTI fix notes.
  • Google Chrome: Stable-channel desktop updates 157.0.8103.50/.51 for Windows and macOS and 157.0.8103.50 for Linux.
  • Danfoss AK-SM800A controllers: Refrigeration controllers managed remotely by maintenance firms; firmware through 5.3.2 examined by researchers, with 5.4.0 not fixing the six flaws and 5.4.0 impact not ruled out by the German agency.
  • Optimizely Web Experimentation: Customer project snippets served from cdn.optimizely.com across at least 178 organizations, including media, technology, retail and financial services sites.
  • Fandango checkout pages: Official website checkout exposed from October 8 to 11, 2026, affecting customers who made purchases through Fandango’s website and app.

Attack Vectors and Techniques

  • Cloud-abusing command and control: GraphDoor and GitDoor hide C2 traffic behind Microsoft SharePoint/Microsoft Graph, GitHub repositories, and Ably messaging; traffic is proxied through modified Erfrp clients and WebSocket-based plugins to blend with legitimate enterprise cloud traffic.
  • Initial access and persistence: MeshCentral/MeshAgent abuse, exposed Ray job agents, SSH authorized_keys modification, kernel rootkits, initramfs loaders, and systemd services are used to establish and maintain access.
  • Supply-chain JavaScript injection: Malicious Project JavaScript inserted into Optimizely snippets served crypto wallet address replacement, payment-card skimming, and iOS exploit-kit iframes to visitors of trusted third-party sites.
  • RMM abuse and lateral movement: ScreenConnect authentication bypass and SCKit RelayTrap techniques attempt file transfer and execution on technician workstations during remote support sessions, potentially spreading from customer devices to MSP environments.
  • AI-agent automation: Attackers use ARTEX, CAIRN, CyberStrikeAI and similar multi-agent frameworks to automate vulnerability discovery, patch reverse-engineering, credential hunting, and adaptive attacks against internet-exposed endpoints.
  • Endpoint evasion and execution: DLL sideloading, process injection including APC, credential dumping with Mimikatz and CookieKatz, screenshot capture, keystroke logging, and NTLM authentication to attacker-controlled destinations.

Threat Actor Activities

  • Operation ShadowCloud actor: Unknown threat actor targeting government sectors in Asia and Africa since early 2026; Kaspersky notes tactical overlap with Bronze Sandcastle, FamousSparrow and CopperTurtle/BlackTech but stops short of proving a direct link. Activity deploys GraphDoor and GitDoor backdoors for cloud-based C2 and plugin-based post-exploitation.
  • REF3364 / SANSAR cryptomining cluster: Financially motivated activity observed in late October 2026 targeting Linux GPU infrastructure; Elastic reports Turkish language comments in rootkit source code, but the operator is not clearly identified. Techniques include exposed Ray job access, NVML wrapper telemetry falsification, kernel rootkit concealment, and an Ethereum smart-contract-resolved backdoor.
  • 8-Digits Team, INC Ransom and Lynx: Extradited Kazakh national Roman Khlynovskiy is alleged to have participated in ransomware attacks against U.S. schools, hospitals and healthcare entities, with over $500 million in ransom demands and claims of more than 70 million health records. INC and Lynx leak sites list education, healthcare, government and industrial victims.
  • AI agent and framework actors: Financially motivated and suspected state-sponsored groups are using ARTEX, CAIRN, CyberStrikeAI, HexStrike AI and related agentic frameworks; suspected North Korean actors have used Claude Code, and UNC6836 with suspected Midnight Neptune links targeted cryptocurrency firms using modified Claude Code and the SOMBERMEME backdoor. ARTEX was also linked to the Barracuda cloud intrusion and similar activity against Railway.
  • Unknown operators in Optimizely and N-central intrusions: The Optimizely supply-chain actor obtained write access to unrelated customer projects; attribution is not provided. N-central crypto-mining intrusions are tied to exploitation of earlier 4EverAble2Shell flaws, with 21 compromised servers reported across multiple countries by Huntback.
